This guide is an introduction to genomics, with a focus on cancer, written by and for
computer scientists and engineers. Its goal is to give you a working mental model of the
field as quickly as possible, and that comes with a few caveats. Please keep them in
mind as you read.

- **The guide paints in broad strokes.** The biology underlying cancer genomics is
  exceedingly complex and takes years of study, so we draw abstractions around a lot of
  it to keep it approachable. Please be forgiving if you feel we have glossed over your
  favorite quirk of cancer genomics.
- **That said, simplified does not mean wrong.** Nothing in this guide should be
  incorrect. If you find something that is, please [file an issue on the GitHub
  repository](https://github.com/stjude/learngenomics.dev/issues).
- **It covers eukaryotic biology.** Everything here refers to the molecular biology of
  organisms whose DNA is enclosed within a nucleus. Most familiar species are
  eukaryotes; the main exceptions are bacteria and archaea, whose DNA sits loose in the
  cell. At times, the guide focuses specifically on sequencing human cells.
- **It is for research use only.** Everything in the guide is presented within a
  research context and may not be relied on in making decisions about patients. Please
  see [the LICENSE.md](https://github.com/stjude/learngenomics.dev/blob/main/LICENSE.md)
  for more information.

This guide is maintained by its community, and contributions of every size are welcome,
from fixing a typo to writing a new page. Every page has an "Edit this page" link at the
bottom, and you can open issues and pull requests on [the GitHub
repository](https://github.com/stjude/learngenomics.dev). We hope you enjoy the read!
